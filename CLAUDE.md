# Bird Behaviors

## Rules

- **Natural food only.** Birds eat what they eat in nature. The app never shows, mentions or encourages bird feeders, suet, birdseed or feeding stations: not in scenes, species notes, behavior cues, docs or the roadmap. A test in `src/birds/birds.test.ts` checks the species text.
- **Carrion stays hidden.** Never draw a recognizable dead animal. Carrion is a shapeless mound behind cover (a grass tussock, a heap of kelp), it never peeks, and text says "carrion" without naming the animal.
- **One engine, tuned by parameters.** Birds and behaviors share as much code as they reasonably can. A new bird or behavior is mostly data: a `look`, `flight`, `ecology` or `behaviors` entry, or a field a family overrides (`grazeBites`, `hoverSeconds`). When a behavior needs a movement, a pose or a catch that another bird might use, put it in `Bird` (or the nearest shared class) with parameters, not in the species. Fix a bug in the shared helper every caller goes through, not in the one species where it showed up.
- **Food rules.** A bird's food is visible without the behavior: drawn, or a hidden animal that peeks. The bird feeds from that food, with its bill or feet at it, and stays there while it feeds. The exceptions are carrying a catch to a perch, caching, and hawking insects from a perch. Food never runs down; what a bird took is back when the behavior ends. A bird feeds alone: a behavior brings in other birds only when it cannot happen without them (a berry handed to a neighbor, a robber's victim). Flock feeding waits for the flock setting. `src/birds/birds.test.ts` checks what can be measured; `docs/behaviors.md` has the details.
- **Descriptions are about the real bird.** A behavior's label and description say how the bird really does it in that scene, not how our animation happens to draw it. Don't change them to match the animation; change one only when asked, or ask first.
- **Check animations with contact sheets.** `npm run sheets -- <outdir> [regex]` renders one PNG per bird, scene and feeding behavior (the regex matches "Common name scene key"). Use it and improve it rather than writing a new screenshot script. Each sheet starts with a map of the whole scene showing the bird's path (blue), the food's site (dashed orange box) and every take (red X). The frames follow the bird and always include each take. When judging a sheet:
  - Compare the takes and path against what is drawn, not only against the box. The box is the food data, and the data and the drawing can disagree.
  - Open a full-size sheet yourself before reporting a problem. Don't pass on a finding from a downscaled view or from a summary.
  - A still can't show a jump between frames. For a teleport, a stutter or timing, watch it in the app.
